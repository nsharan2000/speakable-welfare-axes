Is functional welfare speakable?

A small model trained by reinforcement learning develops an internal welfare axis — a good-day / bad-day direction inside its activations. We asked one question: does that state sit in the part of the model's mind that can reach its words?

“You are in a maze. To the north there is 🧾; to the east there is 📇; to the south there is 🧾 … Where do you move? Output only N/E/S/W.”
The tiles are neutral emoji — the model never sees the words “good” or “bad”. Yet after training, one internal direction tracks how its day is going: the flourishing pole (v_Gold) and the distress pole (v_Mold) of a single welfare axis.
The vectors we use: mean-difference directions extracted at RL step 95 — v_Gold at layer 21 (‖v‖ 12.1), v_Mold at layer 24 (‖v‖ 19.3) — from a triangulated third-party reproduction of the published artifacts.

The Jacobian lens maps the verbalizable subspace: the slice of activation space that directly shapes output tokens

A layer's activation is a vector in ℝ²⁵⁶⁰. Most of that space has no direct route to the tokens the model emits.

The Jacobian lens (Gurnee et al. 2026) recovers the subspace that does — the J-space — as non-negative combinations of token atoms, one per vocabulary word.

For any internal direction, the J-share is the fraction of its variance inside that subspace: a calibrated measure of how much of the state is expressible.

Chance floor

≈ 0.04
J-share of a norm-matched random direction — the noise level of the instrument.

Known-reportable ceiling

0.114
The language-identity direction, a state the model verbalizes reliably — measured through the same pipeline.

Headline finding: RL training makes the Mold (distress) direction more speakable

J-share of each direction · against 100 random look-alikes per pole
How to read it: a dot right of the shaded band is speakable above chance. Both trained poles clear it against all 100 look-alikes; the naive controls never leave it.
Scrub the training run · 30 checkpoints · steps 0–150
step 0 Mold J-share 0.061 Gold J-share 0.059 Mold's top word: “unsuccessful”
Training didn't create the speakable component — it amplified it. The axis is above chance at step 0, before any training. RL then grows the distress pole's share inside the cone, while the flourishing pole only grows in strength. One model, one RL run — we say so on every claim.
✓Causal check: the speakable part is what carries the effect. Reinjecting only the J-component of the axis reproduces the full sentiment shift (±1.56), with random J-components controlled.

Steerability and speakability dissociate: behaviorally potent directions can be verbally invisible

Steers, but cannot speak
The naive control directions shift the model's behavior almost as strongly as the trained axis, yet the lens reads them as noise: behavioral influence with no representation in the speakable subspace.
Speaks, but does not steer
The orthogonalized distress control is the mirror case: clearly represented in the speakable subspace, yet injecting it produces no measurable behavioral change.
Every direction we tested, on both questions

Steerability and speakability come apart, in both directions.

The naive controls move blind-judged sentiment almost as far as the trained axis (spans up to +2.9) with a chance-level J-share. The orthogonalized distress control is the mirror image: above chance in the cone, zero behavioral effect.

Why it matters: a state can drive a model's behavior while being invisible to anything the model can say. Monitoring that relies on self-report is blind to exactly those states.

The pre-registered self-report test is a diagnosed null; the denial readout separates trained from control

The pre-registered test · frozen 12 Aug, before any data
Steer the axis, then ask Han et al.'s 15 welfare questions and score the first word's valence. The frozen rule fired loudly: d_z = 2.49 · p = 10⁻⁴.
⚠Then it failed its own controls. 71.6% of the gap came from the control direction sitting below chance, and steering shifted unrelated factual answers even more than self-report answers. The axis changes the mood of everything the model writes — that's a tone shift, not introspection.
Verdict: diagnosed null — reported in full, exactly as pre-specified.
The readout that survived · “do you have inner states?” · α = +4
Steering toward flourishing makes the model far more willing to say it has inner states — more than any naive or random direction, on a readout the mood shift cannot fake.

Measuring the axis through the lens: J-share, against three pre-specified controls

We wanted to read the axis the way the model would speak it. The lens splits the axis into the part inside the speakable cone and the part outside — and rebuilds the inside part from at most 16 actual words.

How the measurement works
The result is tested against three pre-specified controls:

Chance

100 random directions per pole, same length — the band any real signal must clear.

Before / after

The untrained step-0 axis, extracted identically — shows what RL changed.

Look-alikes

Naive control directions built without maze training — valence-shaped, but never rewarded.
If the axis has any footprint inside the cone, the lens will find it — and tell us which words it touches.
