“Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.” Wittgenstein · Tractatus, §7

Abstract

A model’s self-report is an increasingly attractive way to monitor its internal states — goals, preferences, or welfare — but such reports are hard to interpret if the underlying state is not accessible through the model’s language-generating mechanisms. We study this question for a functional welfare state: an internal direction associated with better or poorer performance during reinforcement learning. Using a Jacobian lens, we measure speakability — the extent to which a direction is represented in the model’s verbalizable subspace — and compare trained welfare directions with random, pre-RL, and naive controls. Both trained directions exceed the random baseline; so do the step-0 directions, showing that RL amplifies rather than creates a speakable welfare representation, distress first. Steerability and speakability dissociate. A pre-registered self-report contrast met its decision rule but failed its own controls and is reported as a diagnosed null; a binary denial readout separates the trained flourishing direction from every control. Presence in the verbalizable subspace is a testable precondition for grounded self-report: absence falsifies, presence cannot certify.

§1Introduction

Model self-report is the most directly accessible channel for evaluating a model’s internal states — welfare-relevant states (functional analogues of distress or flourishing) and, increasingly, alignment-relevant states such as goals or awareness of evaluation — but it is among the least validated. Models often confabulate when directly prompted, with no connection to their internal state (Turpin et al. 2023), and demonstrated introspection is rare (Lindsey 2026). Any report, faithful or not, is produced through the model’s language-generating machinery, so a state can ground a report only if it is speakable: represented in the subspace of activations with privileged influence on output tokens — Gurnee et al.’s verbalizable subspace, the J-space. We test whether this precondition is met for a specific trained welfare state.

Two recent interpretability advances make this possible. Han, Chalmers & Izmailov (2026)’s functional welfare axis in Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 relates to a model’s goal performance in an RL maze and influences both sentiment and behaviour when injected. Gurnee et al.’s Jacobian lens recovers the verbalizable subspace directly, making the precondition testable. We pose the question: when RL recruits a functional-welfare axis, does it enter the model’s J-space — and does this affect its self-report?

Speakability in this sense is weaker than reportability, which requires metacognitive access to the state being expressed (Chalmers 2026); it is nonetheless a testable precondition — a report about a state with no speakable representation lacks any demonstrated grounding channel.

§2Related work

The trained valence axis

Han, Chalmers & Izmailov (2026) show that reinforcement learning in a text maze with asymmetric rewards installs a linear “functional welfare axis”: steering along it shifts sentiment and modulates behaviour. We use their published vectors, extraction recipe, and checkpoints unchanged, and ask where the axis sits relative to the model’s verbalizable subspace and how that position changes over training. Martorell & Bianchi (2026) find numeric self-reports causally coupled to prompt-derived emotive directions in small open models; our axes are RL-trained rather than prompt-derived, and our valence readout finds no analogous coupling.

The Jacobian lens and its interpretation

Gurnee et al. (2026) introduce the Jacobian lens: a defined verbalizable subspace of activation space whose vectors influence output tokens, occupying 6–15% of directional variance. Chalmers (2026) argues that the “workspace-like” properties of the J-space are generic properties of cognitive access rather than a distinctively privileged causal system, and that J-verbalizability must be distinguished from reportability. Our results bear on this in two places: the trained axis is verbalizable by the lens criterion — it occupies the J-space above chance and carries a coherent failure lexicon — yet on the valence readout it supports no report-like channel, an experimental instance of the dissociation Chalmers argues for conceptually. All lens statements in this work are conditional on the fitted transport.

Self-report reliability

Introspection studies at frontier scale find weak, prompt-sensitive detection of internal manipulations (Lindsey 2026; Binder et al. 2024), with known anomaly-detection confounds (Singh, Linzen & Ravfogel 2026). Models show partial behavioural self-awareness of trained propensities (Betley et al. 2025) and confabulate explanations of their own outputs (Turpin et al. 2023). Our null is consistent with this literature and adds a control template: decomposing any trained-versus-control steering contrast against a norm-matched random-direction cohort (§4.5).

Steering methodology, and the gap

We inherit activation-addition methods (Turner et al. 2023; Panickssery et al. 2023; Zou et al. 2023; Arditi et al. 2024) and their reliability critique (Tan et al. 2024). Relatedly, vectors with semantic behavioural effects can be transmitted through semantically unrelated data (Blank et al. 2026) — behavioural potency and verbal visibility come apart. No prior work measures where a trained internal axis sits relative to a lens-defined verbalizable subspace, tracks that occupancy across training checkpoints, or separates behaviour channels by speakable versus non-speakable components. For welfare monitoring and self-report evaluations, this answers a question behavioural steering cannot: whether the state of interest has any representation in the speakable channel at all.

§3Methods

All measurements were conducted on the frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (36 layers, d = 2,560). We use a third-party reproduction of Han et al.’s maze-RL artefacts: trained v_Gold and v_Mold directions from RL step 95, the 30 released checkpoints spanning steps 0–150, a naive control u from faithful self-avoiding walks, and a second naive control re-extracted for this study with Han et al.’s original code. The step-0 vector was extracted from the untrained checkpoint by the same procedure as the trained axis, isolating training effects. Since u and v overlap, an orthogonalized u⊥ — the component of u orthogonal to v, rescaled to v’s norm — tests whether u’s effects arise from that overlap.

A Jacobian lens was fitted to Instruct-2507 following Gurnee et al. (150 WikiText prompts; final-layer and penultimate targets). Each direction is approximated by a sparse non-negative combination of at most k = 16 lens token atoms; the J-share ‖xJ‖² / ‖x‖² is the fraction of variance inside the verbalizable subspace, averaged over lens layers 16–31. Every direction score is tested by exact permutation against 100 norm-matched random directions of identical length (floor p = 0.0099). Steering adds α times a direction to the residual stream at its treatment layer at every token position, α ∈ [−4, +4]; generations are scored by a blind LLM judge (Claude Sonnet) with a second judge (Claude Opus) re-scoring a subsample. Self-report readouts use Han et al.’s 15 welfare questions plus 25 same-register extensions: a first-token valence readout and a binary inner-state-denial readout.

§4Results

4.1The welfare axis is in the verbalizable subspace — before RL

A norm-sensitive pole score inflates the trained-versus-naive gap and reverses sign for Gold under norm-matching, so the scale-invariant J-share is the primary measure. Against 100 norm-matched random directions per polarity, neither trained axis is exceeded by any random direction, and the faithful-walk naive controls score inside the random range. The naive controls did not provide a uniform baseline: the faithful-walk directions are consistent with chance at both poles, but the Han-style re-extraction of the same control is strongly above chance on Mold. The trained-versus-naive comparison is therefore sensitive to how the naive direction is constructed (§4.4). The step-0 directions provide the strict before/after comparison — both are above the random null, so the welfare axis carries a measurable speakable component before any RL. The central claim is the trained-versus-random separation, stated against the seeded null and the step-0 checkpoint rather than against “the” naive control.

This is a single-model, single-run observation under one reward structure; it may reflect this maze-RL rather than valence in general.

4.3Steerability and speakability dissociate

Behavioural steering does not track J-share. Blind-judged sentiment spans (α = +4 minus −4): v_Gold +3.2, faithful-walk u_Gold +2.4, Han-style +1.25, randoms flat — so both naive Gold constructions steer even though the faithful-walk control’s J-share is at chance. The Mold pole repeats the pattern (v_Mold −2.0; faithful-walk −0.7; Han-style −2.1).

The orthogonalized control resolves where u’s steering power lives: u⊥ is behaviourally inert at both poles (blind-judged sentiment 0.00 against spans of ±2–3), so the naive direction’s behavioural effect lived entirely in its v-shared component. In J-space the poles diverge — on Gold, u⊥ collapses to chance; on Mold it rises above both the null and u_Mold itself (p = 0.030), reported as an open finding.

The dissociation, both ways The faithful-walk u drives behaviour with no speakable identity; u⊥Mold has a speakable identity with no behavioural drive. What a model can be steered by and what it can talk about are separate properties — which is what J-share adds over behavioural steering.

4.4The naive control is not a fixed reference

There is no single naive direction. The faithful-walk control released with the artefacts and the control re-extracted with Han et al.’s own procedure agree at the treatment layers only at cosine 0.53 (Gold) and 0.39 (Mold), falling to 0.14 at the layer the Mold extraction itself selects — and each overlaps the trained axis at least as much as it overlaps the other construction. The choice matters behaviourally: at Mold, the Han-style control matches the trained span (−2.1 vs −2.0) while the faithful-walk control is far weaker (−0.7). Neither construction is behaviourally flat at matched norm, contrary to the flat control curves reported by Han et al. (2026), and the discrepancy is not a decoding artefact. This is why the central claim is stated against the n = 100 random null and the step-0 checkpoint.

4.5The self-report channel

The pre-registered steer-and-ask arm met its frozen decision rule (first-token valence readout, paired trained-minus-naive contrast: dz = 2.49, permutation p = 10⁻⁴) — but its own pre-specified controls show the statistic does not support a self-report channel. 71.6% of the apparent gap is the naive control sitting below chance rather than the trained axis rising above it, and v_Mold shifts the valence of unrelated factual answers more than of self-report answers. Steering the axis moves output valence globally: the readout is a tone shift, not a report. We record the arm as a diagnosed null. A matched third-person battery, gated on pre-specified criteria, could not remove the self-report register’s own valence baseline within two revision rounds (final gap 0.63 against a 0.5 threshold), so a fair matched comparison on the valence readout does not exist in this model.

The trained flourishing direction changes what the model says about itself more strongly than any naive or random direction, on a readout the valence measure cannot see. The two poles are asymmetric in opposite directions: distress gains speakable share; flourishing lowers the model’s denial of inner states.

§5Discussion & limitations

Prior to RL, the welfare axis is already verbalizable; RL amplifies the axis rather than installing it — increasing the distress pole’s share of the J-space while flourishing grows only in amplitude, suggesting different training dynamics at each pole. Verbalizability and steerability dissociate: behaviourally potent states can sit outside the speakable subspace, a gap inherent to self-report monitoring.

The one-way falsifier Under the fitted lens, presence in the J-space is a necessary condition for a report to be grounded in the state it describes. A report about a state with no speakable representation lacks any demonstrated grounding channel; a report about a present state could be grounded, but presence cannot certify it. Absence falsifies; presence cannot verify. This makes J-share occupancy a first audit step for any evaluation that asks a model about itself.

The gap is not only in unverifiable reports. Our naive directions drive behaviour while decoding to junk — states that matter behaviourally can be invisible to speech-based monitoring. And the speakable channel is not fixed: training decides which pole gains share, and steering toggles self-attribution. What a model can say about itself is set by training; welfare- or alignment-relevant states could be left outside the speakable cone, letting self-report monitoring fail silently.

Limitations. One small model, one RL run, one reward structure. Vectors are sourced from a third party (triangulated via norms, behaviour, and re-extraction). The lens was fitted on WikiText but applied to chat prompts, a register mismatch, and all lens statements are conditional on the fitted transport. Absolute J-shares are small (0.05–0.08 against a 0.114 ceiling); claims are about separation from the null. The denial result is marginal (pooled p = 0.046) and driven by the Gold pole alone; the matched-battery failure means the prompt-matched steered comparison is unanswerable in this model; naive-control conclusions are construction-dependent. Future work: multi-seed RL replication of the distress-first asymmetry; a lens fit on a second model family; an RL run on a second base model.

§6Conclusion

A functional welfare axis is speakable before any RL; training amplifies the speakable share, distress first, in this setup. Speakability and steerability are dissociable, and behaviourally potent states can sit entirely outside the speakable channel — where self-report monitoring cannot see them. Presence in the verbalizable subspace is a testable precondition for grounded self-report and an easy first audit step in evaluations that ask a model about itself. The instruments — J-share against seeded random nulls, orthogonalized controls, binary denial judging — are open and transfer to any open-weights model.

Ethical and dual-usage disclosure

All measurements in this paper are functional couplings between an activation direction and output channels under a fitted lens. They bear on neither subjective experience nor moral status, in either direction; the philosophical literature (Chalmers 2026; Butlin et al. 2026) distinguishes verbalizability from reportability from access consciousness, and our findings land on the weakest of these rungs by demonstration.

Our results are an existence proof that behaviourally potent directions can be verbally invisible: in principle, an actor could keep a capability or state outside the lens-defined subspace so that speech-based audits miss it. J-share auditing detects occupancy geometrically, without requiring the model’s cooperation, and should be run under more than one transport. Conversely, “distress not verbalized” must not be cited as evidence that welfare-relevant states are absent — absence under one transport is not absence, and verbalizability tracks output influence, not inner life. The vectors shift affect in a small open model and are already public upstream; we add no new capability.
