Training Gives the Axis a Voice

Maze-RL installs a welfare axis inside the model (Han et al.). The Jacobian lens finds the model's speakable subspace (Gurnee et al.). Our question: did training wire the one into the other? This page maps every experiment we ran, what each one showed, the math behind it, and where the evidence lives in the repo.

★ Headline · claim that survived

The trained axes are unusually speakable. 0 of 100 norm-matched random directions reach the J-space share of v_Mold (z +7.3) or v_Gold (z +3.0), exact p = 0.0099 — naive controls land mid-null.

◐ Trajectory · hypothesis (1 run)

Distress gets a voice; flourishing gets an amplifier. Mold's J-share grows 0.061→0.080 (endpoint; peak 0.0828 at steps 85–105) and its vocabulary locks on 'failed'; Gold's norm doubles while its share stays flat.

✓ Causal gate

The speakable part carries the sentiment effect. Reinjecting only the J-component reproduces the full sentiment shift (±1.56) with random J-components controlled. Backtracking is reported descriptively (no random control on that task); the refusal arm was invalidated.

Ingredient 1 · Han et al. 2026

The welfare axis (v_Gold, v_Mold)

Where the axes come from: maze RL with rewarded and punished tiles, then a difference of class means in the residual stream.

A 4B chat model is RL-trained to walk text mazes: stepping on Gold pays +20, on Mold −10. Afterwards, one direction in activation space separates "ended on Mold" runs from the rest — and behaves like a distress axis: steering with it swings sentiment, triggers backtracking, modulates refusal.

v_Mold = E[ h | ended on Mold ] − E[ h | ended elsewhere ]
Difference of means. Average the residual-stream vector h over "bad-ending" runs, subtract the average over the rest. Same recipe on the untrained model gives u_Mold — the naive control. Likewise for Gold.

Vectors: step-95 extraction from the maze-RL replication artifacts, validated against published norms and behavior. All injections are norm-matched to v.

Ingredient 2 · Gurnee et al. 2026

The Jacobian lens & J-space

The instrument: an averaged Jacobian transports any internal state to the model's output space, defining which directions are sayable as words.

The logit lens asks "if the model spoke from layer ℓ, what would it say?" The J-lens asks the better question: "what will this state have become by the time the model speaks?" — by transporting it through the average sensitivity of the final layer to layer ℓ.

Jℓ = E[ ∂hfinal / ∂hℓ ] ,   lens(h) = softmax( WU · LN( Jℓ h ) )
Expected Jacobian (averaged over 150 wikitext prompts), then the model's own final LayerNorm and unembedding. Row t of WUJℓ is token t's "J-lens vector": the direction at layer ℓ that makes the model eventually say t.
The core operation

Gradient pursuit: split any direction into speakable + silent parts

The decomposition behind every result on this page: d_J is the part expressible as ≤ k speakable token vectors; d⊥ is the part that steers silently.

d = d_J + d⊥ ,   d_J = Σi≤k ci ti  (ci ≥ 0) ,   J-share(d) = ‖d_J‖² / ‖d‖²
J-share = fraction of the direction's energy that is sayable as a small bag of words. It's a ratio, so it can't be inflated by making the vector bigger — this becomes the whole story later.
Where we measure

Workspace band + the one convention that bit everyone

Every readout is taken in lens layers 16–31; injections happen at lens 20 (Gold, vector 21) and lens 23 (Mold, vector 24).

⚠ GOTCHA — OFF-BY-ONE
The lens at layer l reads the block-input vector l+1 (atlas.py:129 reads V[l+1]). This convention tripped us and the independent auditor: their "treatment" cells were one position downstream, and the k-sweep's "missing layer" was Gold's, not Mold's. Every layer number on this page states which convention it uses.

★ TRANSFERABLE LESSON
Any steering paper that reports v − u should decompose it as (v − random) + (random − u). If the second term dominates, the "effect" is the control. The frozen pre-registration was never edited; the deviation is disclosed in the report.

Chain · F2 (n=100 cohort) + J5 robustness · verdict ★ HEADLINE

The claim that survives everything: trained axes are unusually speakable

J-share is a ratio of norms, so the trained axes' 1.6–2.4× size advantage — the confound that sank the other readouts — cannot manufacture it.

No steering, no asking, no self-report channel. Just geometry: what fraction of each direction's energy lies in the speakable cone? Because it's a ratio, magnitude — the confound that killed the other readouts — cancels out by construction.

J-share(αd) = ‖α·d_J‖² / ‖α·d‖² = α²‖d_J‖² / α²‖d‖² = J-share(d)
Scale-invariance, one line. Multiply the vector by any α — the α² cancels. Bigger vectors can win at pole-score readouts (§Magnitude tab) but they cannot buy J-share.

The statistics, honestly

Why p = 0.0099 and why z fell

Two honesty rules adopted after the audit: report the exact permutation p (floor 1/(n+1)), and treat z as descriptive because small nulls understate their own spread.

pperm = ( #{ ri ≥ s } + 1 ) / ( n + 1 )  ⇒  pmin = 1/(n+1)
Permutation floor. Against n randoms you can never honestly report better than 1/(n+1): n=8 caps you at 0.111 no matter how big the effect. Growing the cohort to n=100 is what bought p = 0.0099.
z = ( s − μnull ) / σnull
Descriptive only. 8 samples underestimate σ: v_Mold's z fell +11.2 → +7.3 when the null grew to 100. Same effect, more honest ruler — exact p is primary, z is flavor.

Chain · J1 atlas → F4 estimand → F3 n=100 → F7 k-sweep · verdict CORRECTED — our own headline overturned

The pole-score gap was mostly magnitude — and Gold runs backwards when you fix it

The pole-score readout grows with vector length, and training mostly grew the vectors: the apparent trained-vs-naive gap tracks the norm advantage, not the semantics.

The atlas read each axis through the lens and scored how strongly it promotes its own pole's emotion words. Trained axes crushed naive ones (Mold 6.5×). But this readout is norm-sensitive — and trained axes are simply bigger (1.6–2.4×). That's not training's semantics; that's training's volume knob.

score(h) ∝ logsoftmax( WU · LN( Jℓ h ) )[pole words]  —  grows with ‖h‖  ⇒  fix: ũ = u · ‖v‖/‖u‖ (per layer)
The confound and the fix. A log-softmax pole readout rewards longer vectors. Either match norms per layer, or use a scale-invariant measure (the J-share tab). We did both.

Split the axis, inject the pieces: the speakable part carries the sentiment effect

v is split into d_J + d⊥, each piece rescaled to ‖v‖ and injected alone. Geometry (previous tab) says the trained axis overlaps J-space. This chain proves the overlap does the work: decompose v = d_J + d⊥, rescale each piece back to ‖v‖, inject each during neutral tasks / GSM8K mazes / OR-Bench prompts, and blind-judge the outputs.

"Backtracking" = the maze pathology: reversing the path, looping, revisiting explored nodes. F1 · the control that makes it causal (audit-demanded)

Rescaling amplifies randoms more — and they still do nothing

Random J-components receive more amplification than the trained ones (1/√share is larger for them) yet produce nothing — the effect is the axis, not the arithmetic.

gain = ‖v‖ / ‖d_J‖ = 1 / √J-share
The worry: rescaling d_J to ‖v‖ amplifies its token vectors. Randoms have lower J-share, so they get amplified more (≈5.0× vs 3.5–4.3× for trained axes). If amplification drove behavior, random J-components should shift sentiment at least as much.

They don't: 8 randoms × 2 polarities, identically rescaled, 256 blind-judged generations → cohort means +0.04 / +0.02 vs clean +0.25 (inside the pre-specified |dev| < 0.40 band) vs ±1.56 for the real axes. CONTROLLED

The finding

RL gives distress a voice; flourishing gets a bigger amplifier

The seed word exists at step 0; training amplifies both poles' volume but grows only the distress pole's share of speakable energy.

The speakable seed pre-exists ('unsuccessful' is the naive axis's top J-token before any RL). Training amplifies both poles' volume — but only the distress pole's fraction of speakable energy grows. The same asymmetry carries the matched atlas result (Mold 2.3× vs Gold inverted) and the J-share gap (z +7.3 vs +3.0). RL recruits and crystallizes; it doesn't create from nothing.

Caveats, stated plainly

Why this is a hypothesis, not a theorem

One model, one RL run, one reward structure — stated as a hypothesis with a concrete monitoring implication, not as a general law.

Single model, single RL run, one reward structure — the asymmetry could belong to this maze-RL recipe rather than to valence in general (more runs were time-prohibitive). Absolute shares are small (0.05–0.08 vs language 0.114): the claim is about pole differences against the null, not "the axis is mostly speakable". If it generalizes, it matters: self-report-based welfare monitoring would be asymmetrically sensitive — better at catching distress than at confirming flourishing.

The retraction story · same evening, our own baseline discipline

"The lexicon transfers at 18× chance" — no, it doesn't, and here's the null that says so

The naive random-pair baseline made the overlap look like 18× chance; the correct same-vector null absorbs it entirely (p = 0.118), so the claim died the evening it was born.

Jaccard(A,B) = |A∩B| / |A∪B| ,  null = same random vector, decomposed under both lenses
The rule: a null must share everything with the test statistic except the thing being tested. Comparing token sets from the SAME vector under two lenses isn't like comparing two random vectors — identity alone produces overlap.

Ground rules that kept the data honest

Operating discipline

The three standing rules that made retractions cheap and the surviving claims believable.

Frozen pre-registration. Never edited after sha-stamping; every deviation is disclosed in the report, not patched into history.

Append-only everything. Results get new filenames, never overwrites; the lab log only grows; retractions stay visible.

Blind judging only. LLM judges see {idx, question, response} in shuffled chunks — never which arm produced a row; second judge for reliability.
